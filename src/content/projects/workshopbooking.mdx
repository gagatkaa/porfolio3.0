## Overview

**Code Runners** is a workshop-booking experience created around an unusual combination: **programming and freerunning**.

The workshop invites programmers and gamers to step away from their screens and take on a physical urban challenge, combining movement with small problem-solving and coding-inspired tasks.

The goal was not simply to design a booking form, but to make the **booking process feel like the beginning of the experience itself**.

<Image src={hero} alt="Code Runners booking experience" />

</div>

<div class="case-section">

## The concept

The original workshop brief focused on freerunning, adrenaline and exploring the city of Kortrijk.

I reinterpreted it for a tech-oriented audience through the idea of **Code Runners** — participants moving through the city while combining physical challenges with the mindset of problem solving.

The booking flow adapts the workshop to the user by collecting information such as:

- number of participants
- preferred date and duration
- fitness and experience level
- occasion
- food and drink extras
- personal information

Each decision becomes part of one continuous interactive journey rather than a conventional checkout form.

</div>

<div class="case-section">

## Finding the visual direction

The concept went through several visual directions before reaching the final result.

My early exploration was heavily influenced by gaming and *Mirror's Edge*. After feedback, I moved away from that direction and began exploring **Bauhaus graphics, urban architecture and editorial collage**.

<Image src={styleboard} alt="Code Runners styleboard" />

The final system combines black-and-white archival imagery with orange accents, geometric lines and monospace typography inspired by programming interfaces.

This gave the project a visual language that connects **technology, architecture and movement** without relying too heavily on gaming references.

</div>

<div class="case-section">

## Booking as interaction

A key part of the brief was continuous feedback during the booking process.

I introduced a vertical progress indicator that moves with the user through each stage, making it clear where they are and how much of the experience remains.

<Image src={wireframes2} alt="Code Runners booking flow" />

Inputs are integrated directly into the visual composition — from participant selection and skill levels to occasions and extras — so the interface feels less like filling in a form and more like configuring your own workshop.

<Image src={wireframes3} alt="Code Runners booking configuration" />

Pricing and selections remain visible throughout the flow, keeping the experience playful without sacrificing clarity.

</div>

<div class="case-section">

## Visual system

The interface uses a deliberately limited palette of **orange, black, white and grey**, allowing photography and typography to carry most of the personality.

I combined:

- architectural photography
- archival computer imagery
- collage
- geometric line work
- programming-inspired typography
- minimal UI controls

<Image src={wireframes4} alt="Code Runners UI detail" />

Reducing the amount of color compared with my earlier concepts helped the final design feel more focused while keeping its urban energy.

</div>

<div class="case-section">

## Outcome

Code Runners turned a functional booking task into an extension of the workshop itself.

The project taught me how much **concept, visual identity and interaction design need to support the same experience** — and how simplifying an early idea can often make the final direction much stronger.

Instead of designing a form around the workshop, I designed the booking journey to already make the user feel like they were entering the world of **Code Runners**.

</div>
